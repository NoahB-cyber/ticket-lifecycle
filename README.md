<br />
 Step 1: Creation - The ticket enters the system by manually entering it on the osTicket website, over the phone, or email.
  

  Step 2: Triage - This is where the ticket gets observed and adjusted to identify priority, severity, cost, category, and gets authorized/approved
  
  Step 3: Dispatch - This is where the ticket is scheduled properly according to issue type, severity, available tech, and work location, weather that would be an onsite issue or remote
  
  Step 4: Resolution - This final step is where the ticket actually gets worked on, meaning the issue getting validated, as much information as possible gets gathered, a schedule is made with the end user, and escalation is adjusted correctly.

With all these steps in mind its very important to remember that keeping in touch with the end user and communicating throughout the process is very important, reach out with and without information to remind them that you and/or your team are working to resolve the issue and will continue to keep them updated.

After a ticket has been resolved or closed, update the ticket to "closed" or "resolved", depending on your companies policy, and input all data required. 

Obviously, getting the actual issue itself taken care of is important, but it is also crucially important to keep in touch with end users, making them feel heard, in the loop, and assured that you and others are actively trying to fix their problem.
